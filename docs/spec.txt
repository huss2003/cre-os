1. The decision
Build a personally controlled operating layer with a relational database, a small phone-friendly workbench, and
approved connections to the tools you already use. Keep Outlook, Teams, Excel, PowerPoint and the official CRM. Use
ChatGPT for interactive analysis and polished deliverables. Use metered API calls for unattended processing. Start with
one AI provider. Add a second only after a measured quality or resilience benefit.

The recommended starting point is the low-cost hybrid, budgeted at about ₹7,186/month before tax, including your
existing Plus subscription, under the workload assumptions in section 15. Allow about ₹10,186/month with a modest
maintenance allowance. This uses Supabase Pro, one small DigitalOcean server, and a small custom application. The
professional three-person scenario is about ₹17,808/month before maintenance and tax. These are budgets, not vendor
quotes.

Your desired “team of 15” should be represented by specialist workflows sharing one evidence base. Do not buy 15
subscriptions or create 15 independent agents with competing memories. Automate extraction, preparation,
reconciliation, research and reminders; keep you responsible for calls, relationship judgement, meetings, site visits,
outbound messages, negotiations, pricing, policy and signing.

Three conclusions matter immediately:

1. Plus alone can provide a useful assisted desk and scheduled work where supported, but does not include separately
   billed API usage or guarantee a continuously available CRM, inbox listener and shared production database. A
   low-cost system still needs hosting, integration permissions and maintenance.

2. At ₹30 lakh attributed revenue per transaction, 12 transactions yield ₹3.6 crore. ₹5 crore requires 17 transactions, or
   ₹41.67 lakh average attributed revenue across 12 transactions.
3. Personal ownership of generic software, templates and accounts does not establish ownership of employer data,
   licensed research, client records or work product. Separate those rights in the architecture and developer contract.

9. Explainable lead scoring and proactive intelligence
Start with a configurable 100-point priority model: actionable lease/notice timing 25, confirmed size/location fit 15,
verified business trigger 20, relationship/intro strength 15, recent meaningful engagement 15,
budget/authority/process evidence 10. Each component has a rubric, evidence, last-verification date and score version.
Do not put inferred future revenue into every score component and double count it.

Initial scoring rubric: timing 25 for a verified near action/notice deadline, 20 for a verified 3-12-month opportunity with
a viable decision timeline, 10 for 12-24 months, 0 when unknown. Size/location fit 15 for confirmed fit, 8 for a plausible
but unconfirmed fit, 0 for unknown or excluded. Trigger 20 for directly confirmed active office demand, 12 for
corroborated expansion/consolidation evidence, 5 for a weak single signal, 0 for none. Relationship 15 for an active
trusted decision-maker relationship, 10 for an available permissioned warm introduction, 5 for known contact without a
path, 0 for no path. Engagement 15 for a recent substantive reply with next step, 8 for a meaningful conversation
without next step, 3 for older interaction, 0 for none; email tracking pixels alone are not meaningful engagement.
Budget/process 10 when budget and decision path are confirmed, 5 when partially established, 0 when unknown.
Review recency bands after 30 days of actual work. Disqualification and suppression override the total. Suggested
working tiers: 75+ act this week, 50-74 validate/nurture, below 50 enrich or park; deadlines can override tiers.

Timing rubric: confirmed notice/action deadline within 90 days merits immediate attention, even if expiry is farther
away. The requested 3-12-month expiry window receives high priority only after assessing fit-out and decision lead
time. An already-expired lease is “status needs confirmation,” not an automatic hot lead. More distant 12-24-month
opportunities stay in nurture. Missing dates produce an enrichment task rather than a fabricated expiry.

Use three visible measures: priority score, evidence confidence, relationship warmth. Treat a model confidence label
as a workflow heuristic, not calibrated probability. Initial evidence rubric: documentary/current direct confirmation;
credible reported source; corroborated public signal; single unverified report; inference. Keep unverified information
searchable with its label, but require verification before client claims or material updates.

Suggested daily selection: deadline override first; then expected next-stage value relative to effort, capped by available
call/meeting time, with 10-20% space for new prospect exploration. Show why each selected target displaced another.
Avoid a sophisticated opaque optimisation formula until actual time and conversion data exist.

Research tiers: 50-100 critical accounts weekly and on strong signals; 400-500 active/nurture companies monthly or on
events; 1,000-1,500 universe quarterly and on credible public triggers. Review the broader 8,000-company store mainly
when new evidence arrives. A promise to deeply re-research 25,000 contacts every week is incompatible with low
spend and lawful source access.

Refresh proposals: live shortlisted availability/quotes before use and weekly while active; active opportunities after
every meaningful interaction; top stakeholder employment monthly/manual alert driven; historical transaction facts
preserved with source date; market releases monthly/quarterly; new-asset completion monthly until imminent and
then weekly. These are operating defaults, not source freshness guarantees.

11. Financial and proposal production
Replace ad hoc comparisons with one reusable, auditable model per requirement. Keep a common comparison horizon
and occupation date. Compare conventional bare shell, fitted conventional, managed/flex, renewal, relocation and Jasfo
financing where actual quote terms exist.

Inputs: chargeable and carpet SF, efficiency, planned/installed/occupied seats, utilisation, commencement/fit-out
dates, lock-in/term, notice, base rent, escalation schedule, free-rent months and its conditions, CAM, parking,
power/diesel, HVAC overtime, internet, facility staff/services, insurance where applicable, taxes and recoverability
assumptions, brokerage/legal/move costs, deposit and refund timing, fit-out capex, financing schedule, restoration,
parallel occupation and exit costs. Track quote provenance and validity per input.

Monthly timeline rules:

- Monthly rent = applicable rate × its actual billing area or seat basis, adjusted for the contractual escalation month and
   rent-free conditions. Do not apply an annual increase monthly.
- Monthly discount rate = (1 + annual rate)^(1/12) - 1. Discount dated monthly cash flows consistently; use XNPV-style
   date handling when dates are irregular.
- Deposit outflow at payment and refund at contractual recovery date. A refundable deposit is not full permanent
   expense. Either discount deposit cash flows or separately present opportunity cost, avoiding double counting.
- NPV = sum of cash outflows and inflows discounted from a clearly stated base date. Equivalent monthly cost = NPV /
   monthly annuity factor over the comparison horizon when rate and periods are valid; use NPV/months if rate is zero.

- Show undiscounted TCO, NPV, equivalent monthly cost, ₹/chargeable SF/month, ₹/carpet SF/month and ₹/planned
   seat/month separately. An occupied-seat ratio needs a dated occupancy scenario.
- For tenures shorter than the horizon, include renewal/exit assumptions explicitly. Do not compare a three-year
   package with a five-year total as though the totals are equivalent.

- Financing: principal draw, interest, fees, repayment, default/early termination, fit-out ownership and residual value.
   Do not add financed capex twice as both full upfront capex and principal repayments.

Outputs: clear recommendation and trade-offs, assumptions, cash-flow chart, capex peak, break-even horizon,
renew-vs-relocate, delay/parallel-rent exposure, sensitivity to rent/escalation/utilisation/fit-out/discount rate, and a
decision log. GST, stamp duty, registration, tax credits and accounting treatment require the appropriate finance/legal
inputs; do not hardcode them from generic web estimates. Operational NPV is distinct from lease accounting under Ind
AS 116.

4. Forecasting and requirement estimation
Stage 1 gives descriptive distributions, sourced comparables, expiry cohorts and scenario ranges. Stage 2 uses
consistent quarterly releases, backtests simple baselines and compares them with analyst scenarios. Stage 3 considers
predictive models only after a dated feature history, non-mover comparison group, revisions policy and out-of-time
tests exist.

Vacancy needs vacant stock or occupancy evidence. Do not compute vacancy as 1 - absorption/stock. Net absorption,
gross leasing and supply measure different things. Where definitions match, closing occupied stock = opening occupied
stock + net absorption + documented adjustments; closing stock = opening stock + delivered supply - withdrawals +
coverage adjustments. Derive vacant stock only from reconciled compatible series. Months of supply requires available
marketable stock divided by a compatible monthly take-up measure, with the definition prominently stated; the current
CSVs alone do not establish all those inputs.

Scenario model: geography × quarter × downside/base/upside assumptions for delivery, withdrawals, demand and
rental change. Publish ranges, assumptions and source dates. Treat 2026 as unknown completeness until publisher
metadata resolves actual/YTD/forecast. Report backtest error and prediction intervals; do not call rule scores calibrated
move probabilities.

Seat estimate: eligible headcount × peak simultaneous attendance × (1 + growth allowance), adjusted for shift-sharing
only if confirmed. Area estimate = seats × chosen usable area/seat plus support area, then divide by
carpet-to-chargeable efficiency when translating to chargeable area. State whether area/seat already includes support
area to avoid adding it twice. Use low/base/high attendance and density assumptions with a clear client-unconfirmed
label.

Let r_m = (1+r_a)^(1/12) - 1. NPV of monthly occupancy cost is C_0 + sum C_t/(1+r_m)^t, with positive costs and
negative refunds/inflows. Equivalent monthly occupancy cost = NPV / sum(1+r_m)^-t for t=1…n. If zero discount, use
NPV/n. Contractual cash flows on actual dates use an explicit annual/day-count convention. A time-zero outflow
remains time zero. A rent-free month can still incur CAM, utilities or fees. Deposit opportunity cost is not added again if
its dated outflow/refund are already discounted.

Worked synthetic control, not a supplied quote: 10,000 chargeable SF at ₹80/SF/month for 36 months, no
escalation/free rent/CAM/tax, six-month refundable base-rent deposit at month 0 returned at month 36, zero discount.
Rent ₹800,000/month; nominal rent ₹28.8m; deposit ₹4.8m out and back; total net cost ₹28.8m and equivalent
₹800,000/month. At positive discount, deposit adds economic cost via delayed refund. Adding its full ₹4.8m as expense
would be wrong.

Financing control: ₹10m capex fully lender-paid at time zero, paid back through scheduled principal/interest. Client cash
model uses client contributions and repayments; it must not also count the full ₹10m as an immediate client cash
outflow. A separate project-cost view may show financed capex, clearly distinguished and reconciled to funding.

escalation after 36 months; rent-free excluding CAM; deposit returned late; unequal term/common horizon; early
termination; financed capex; currency mismatch; seat vs SF rate; expired quote; missing cost rating; sensitivity switching
and correct sixth-option coverage. Reconcile first, escalation and final month independently. Generated Excel must
recalculate, exported decks/docs need visual inspection, and every client-ready result needs input-version approval.

Temporal and unit rules
Use UTC instants in storage and Asia/Kolkata presentation/scheduling. Use date-only values for contractual dates.
Preserve date precision and unknown year rather than making a date. Month addition clamps to month-end
consistently with documented EDATE behaviour for integer months; fractional tenure is quarantined for interpretation.
Store numeric SF values with area basis, rupees as decimals and escalation as explicit percentages/fractions with one
convention.

Derived expiry must carry date_basis=calculated_from_source_tenure; contractual-date confirmation supersedes the
canonical value but leaves the old claim visible. A notice deadline additionally needs notice length, event basis and
business/calendar-day rule. Never subtract an assumed notice period and call it contractual.

Transactional update and reversal algorithm
1. Authenticate user/job and establish workspace/classification scope.
2. Resolve source/evidence access; validate patch schema and field authority.
3. Determine risk outside the LLM. Fetch row and lock it; compare expected version.
4. Validate required approval against current patch/content hash and expiry. Reject stale approvals.

5. Write canonical update, increment row version, append audit and task/outbox events in one transaction.
6. Publish private notification from outbox with dedupe key; a retry cannot duplicate the database change.
7. Reversal loads the historical before state and current version, previews dependencies, then applies a compensating
   change. Never roll back unrelated later edits.

Network delivery is at-least-once with idempotent consumers where possible. Do not claim that exactly-once processing
is guaranteed by a retry mechanism. Backfilled interactions must not re-trigger an old outreach sequence or duplicate
hot alerts.

Repair priorities and enrichment backlog
Before any automated action: approve source rights, map schema/period definitions, classify size from row values,
separate event types, resolve building/tenant identities, label source status/as-of, require review of material claims,
block the flawed comparison template, preserve undo and original provenance.

Before client shortlists: verified actual space availability, contiguous floor options, chargeable/carpet basis, current
quotes/inclusions, occupation/fit-out dates, parking/power/technical constraints, authorised landlord/operator contact
and source validity.

Before meaningful pipeline automation: actual current leads, account ownership/conflicts, person contact methods
and permission, stakeholder roles, client-confirmed requirements, next actions, communication history, mandate status
and realistic fee attribution.

Before advanced forecasting: current comparable releases with full-year/YTD/forecast metadata, net/gross absorption
definitions, true vacancy/availability, completions, removals/reclassifications, dated features, non-mover outcomes and
multiple later transaction years. These attachments are a useful starting reference library; they are not a complete
operational CRM or training set.



Import acceptance rules
Reject unknown schema into quarantine, never silently discard. Preserve originals and raw fields. Parse types/units in
staging. Keep historical facts with confidence labels, not false current confirmations. Reconcile observations separately
from unique entities. Re-importing a hash must be idempotent. Revisions create new observations. All fixes occur only
in derived master/staging data or separately authorised copies. Verify all 11 hashes remain unchanged at handover.

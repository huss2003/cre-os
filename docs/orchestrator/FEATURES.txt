MODULE 9: ORCHESTRATOR

Keeping the Entire CRE AI Operating System Working Together

1. MODULE PURPOSE

The Orchestrator is the central coordination and automation layer of the CRE AI Operating System.

Its purpose is to coordinate schedules, dependencies, background processes and activities across all relevant modules.

It ensures that the system operates continuously, processes important updates, triggers the correct workflows, manages priorities and keeps the user informed about important activities.

The Orchestrator should reduce the need for the user to manually operate individual modules.

---

2. CORE FUNCTIONALITIES AND FEATURES

2.1 Automated Workflow Scheduling

The system must automatically schedule and execute recurring and event-based activities.

Features:

- Schedule and run source updates.
- Schedule and execute signal checks.
- Trigger account and opportunity scoring updates.
- Schedule meeting preparation briefs.
- Trigger post-meeting processing.
- Schedule reminders and follow-up activities.
- Generate weekly business reviews.
- Execute recurring background research.
- Manage multiple workflows running simultaneously.
- Allow users to view upcoming, running, completed and failed workflows.
- Allow workflows to be paused and resumed.
- Prevent duplicate execution of the same scheduled action.

2.2 Automated 08:00 Daily Plan

The Orchestrator must generate a daily plan at 08:00.

The daily plan should coordinate existing information and activities across the system.

Features:

- Review scheduled meetings.
- Identify urgent client-related activities.
- Review pending follow-ups and commitments.
- Identify overdue tasks.
- Review high-priority accounts and opportunities.
- Identify important new signals.
- Highlight upcoming meetings requiring preparation.
- Identify tasks that need attention.
- Prioritise activities based on urgency and importance.
- Consider calendar commitments.
- Consider travel requirements.
- Consider the user's daily working capacity.
- Present the most important activities for the day.

The daily plan must still be generated using existing tasks and calendar records when AI services are unavailable.

2.3 Meeting Workflow Automation

The Orchestrator must automatically coordinate activities before and after meetings.

Features:

- Detect upcoming meetings from calendar records.
- Trigger meeting preparation briefs approximately one hour before meetings.
- Route meeting preparation work to the appropriate specialist module.
- Ensure relevant company, contact, property and requirement information is available.
- Trigger post-call processing after meetings.
- Ensure meeting outcomes are processed and relevant records are updated.
- Trigger follow-up tasks based on meeting commitments.
- Schedule reminders for agreed actions.
- Trigger weekly reviews of meeting outcomes and pending commitments.

Meeting cancellation rules:

- When a meeting is cancelled, automatically cancel its associated reminders.
- Prevent obsolete meeting preparation workflows from running.
- Preserve the meeting history and cancellation information.

2.4 Specialist Module Routing

The Orchestrator must route tasks to the appropriate specialist modules instead of attempting to perform every activity itself.

Features:

- Route source updates to the relevant source and research processes.
- Route signal checks to Module 2 – Signal Engine.
- Route scoring updates to Module 3 – Scoring Engine.
- Route contact-related reviews to Module 4 – Contact Enrichment.
- Route outreach-related workflows to Module 5 – Outreach Engine.
- Route meeting preparation and post-meeting processing to Module 7 – Meeting Intelligence.
- Route commercial reassessments to Module 8 – Deal Toolkit.
- Coordinate information retrieval and AI-related tasks with Module 12 – Supercharged Assistant.
- Coordinate updates with Module 1 – Database.
- Track the status of every routed task.
- Track whether the receiving module completed the requested activity.
- Handle failed or delayed module operations.
- Avoid running unnecessary workflows when no relevant change has occurred.

2.5 Event-Driven Automation

The Orchestrator must react to important changes occurring anywhere in the CRE AI Operating System.

Features:

- Detect newly created records.
- Detect updated company information.
- Detect new contact information.
- Detect changes in lease details.
- Detect new rent quotations.
- Detect changes in property requirements.
- Detect meeting creation and cancellation.
- Detect changes in contact employment.
- Detect changes in research information.
- Trigger relevant workflows when these events occur.
- Reassess affected outputs when underlying information changes.
- Ensure that relevant modules receive the updated information.

Example:
When a new rent quotation is added, the Orchestrator should trigger an updated property comparison and warn the user if existing drafts are affected.

2.6 Dependency Management

The Orchestrator must understand relationships between workflows and their dependencies.

Features:

- Identify which workflows depend on other workflows.
- Execute dependent tasks in the correct order.
- Prevent a workflow from executing before its required information is available.
- Identify incomplete dependencies.
- Track pending dependencies.
- Resume workflows when required information becomes available.
- Reassess dependent outputs when source information changes.
- Prevent obsolete tasks from continuing after their underlying requirement changes.

Example:
A property comparison depends on property pricing, CAM, area and lease terms. If a new rent quotation changes the commercial information, the comparison must be reassessed.

2.7 Priority and Daily Capacity Management

The Orchestrator must manage work according to urgency, importance and available capacity.

Features:

- Prioritise urgent client work over routine background research.
- Identify time-sensitive client commitments.
- Consider scheduled meetings and travel requirements.
- Consider the user's daily working capacity.
- Identify conflicting scheduled activities.
- Prevent unnecessary background work from interfering with important client activities.
- Highlight activities that cannot be completed within the available capacity.
- Track delayed and pending work.
- Reprioritise activities when urgent work arrives.

2.8 Reminder and Notification Management

The Orchestrator must coordinate important reminders and notifications.

Features:

- Generate reminders for scheduled meetings.
- Generate reminders for pending commitments.
- Generate reminders for follow-up activities.
- Notify users about important workflow failures.
- Notify users about delayed processes.
- Notify users when important information changes.
- Notify users when a new rent quotation affects an existing comparison.
- Notify users about stale source information.
- Avoid unnecessary or repetitive notifications.
- Prioritise meaningful alerts over routine system updates.

The objective is to provide a small number of meaningful alerts rather than overwhelm the user with notifications.

2.9 Background Job Management

The Orchestrator must manage background processes running across the system.

Features:

- Run source updates in the background.
- Run signal checks in the background.
- Run scoring processes in the background.
- Run research tasks in the background.
- Coordinate enrichment activities.
- Coordinate transcription activities.
- Trigger AI processing tasks.
- Track job execution status.
- Track job completion time.
- Track failed and delayed jobs.
- Track resource and service usage.
- Allow users to inspect background job details.
- Prevent duplicate background actions.

2.10 Failure Recovery and Automatic Retry

The Orchestrator must handle temporary service failures without requiring manual intervention for every failure.

Features:

- Detect failed background jobs.
- Identify temporary service failures.
- Automatically retry temporary failures.
- Avoid duplicate actions during retries.
- Track the number of retry attempts.
- Track the reason for failure.
- Identify workflows that require manual intervention.
- Preserve completed workflow steps when a later step fails.
- Resume eligible workflows after a temporary failure.
- Notify the user when a workflow cannot be recovered automatically.

2.11 System Health and Execution Monitoring

The Orchestrator must provide visibility into the operational status of the system.

Features:

- Track successfully completed workflows.
- Track running workflows.
- Track delayed workflows.
- Track failed workflows.
- Track workflows requiring attention.
- Track source availability.
- Identify unavailable services.
- Identify stale information caused by source outages.
- Track background processing activity.
- Provide a clear execution history.
- Show what succeeded, what is delayed and what needs attention.

2.12 AI and Operational Budget Management

The Orchestrator must manage usage budgets for expensive or resource-intensive activities.

Features:

- Track research usage.
- Track contact enrichment usage.
- Track transcription usage.
- Track AI usage.
- Monitor usage against configured budgets.
- Identify resource-intensive workflows.
- Track usage by workflow or activity.
- Prevent background processes from consuming resources without appropriate limits.
- Provide visibility into operational usage.
- Support configured usage limits.

2.13 Pause and Resume Controls

The user must have direct control over automated operations.

Features:

- Provide a one-click pause control.
- Provide a one-click resume control.
- Allow the user to pause background automation.
- Allow the user to resume paused automation.
- Prevent new eligible jobs from starting while the relevant automation is paused.
- Preserve existing workflow states.
- Resume eligible pending workflows without creating duplicate actions.
- Clearly display whether automation is active or paused.

3. IMPORTANT AUTOMATION RULES

The Orchestrator must follow these rules consistently:

1. NEW RENT QUOTATION:
   When a new rent quotation is added, trigger an updated property comparison and warn the user about affected drafts.

2. CANCELLED MEETING:
   When a meeting is cancelled, cancel its associated reminders and prevent obsolete preparation workflows from running.

3. CONTACT EMPLOYMENT CHANGE:
   When a contact changes employer, trigger a contact review. Do not automatically transfer company opportunities to the new employer.

4. SOURCE OUTAGE:
   When a source becomes unavailable, clearly identify affected information as potentially stale.

5. NEW RECORDS:
   When new records, contacts or lease details are added, update the relevant information and trigger appropriate dependent workflows.

6. AI SERVICE UNAVAILABLE:
   If AI services are unavailable, the daily plan must still be generated using existing tasks and calendar records.

7. EXTERNAL COMMUNICATION:
   External invitations and messages must follow the existing review and manual-send rules. The Orchestrator must not independently send external communications.

8. DUPLICATE PREVENTION:
   Retries and repeated events must not create duplicate actions, reminders or records.

9. PRIORITY MANAGEMENT:
   Urgent client work must take priority over routine background research.

10. INFORMATION DEPENDENCY:
    When underlying information changes, reassess affected outputs instead of continuing to use outdated results.

5. EXPECTED END-TO-END WORKFLOWS

WORKFLOW 1: DAILY BUSINESS PLAN
At 08:00, the Orchestrator reviews calendar records, pending tasks, commitments, priority accounts and important signals. It generates a prioritised daily plan.

WORKFLOW 2: NEW BUSINESS SIGNAL
When a new relevant signal is identified, the Orchestrator coordinates the necessary scoring, contact review and follow-up activities.

WORKFLOW 3: UPCOMING MEETING
Approximately one hour before a meeting, the Orchestrator triggers meeting preparation and ensures that the relevant information is available.

WORKFLOW 4: POST-MEETING PROCESSING
After a meeting, the Orchestrator triggers meeting processing, coordinates relevant database updates and schedules follow-up activities.

WORKFLOW 5: NEW RENT QUOTATION
When a new quotation is added, the Orchestrator triggers an updated commercial comparison and identifies affected drafts.

WORKFLOW 6: CANCELLED MEETING
When a meeting is cancelled, the Orchestrator cancels associated reminders and prevents obsolete meeting workflows from running.

WORKFLOW 7: OVERDUE COMMITMENT
When a commitment becomes overdue, the Orchestrator identifies it and brings it to the user's attention.

WORKFLOW 8: WEEKLY BUSINESS REVIEW
The Orchestrator triggers the weekly review and coordinates the information required from relevant modules.

WORKFLOW 9: TEMPORARY SERVICE FAILURE
When a background process fails because of a temporary service issue, the Orchestrator retries it without duplicating completed actions.

WORKFLOW 10: CONTACT EMPLOYMENT CHANGE
When a contact's employer changes, the Orchestrator triggers a contact review without automatically transferring company opportunities.

---

6. FINAL EXPECTED OUTCOME

The Orchestrator must make the entire CRE AI Operating System function as one coordinated system.

The user should not need to manually check every module, trigger every process or remember every dependency.

The Orchestrator should:

- Coordinate all scheduled and background activities.
- Automatically trigger relevant workflows.
- Keep modules updated when important information changes.
- Prepare the user's daily plan.
- Coordinate meeting preparation and follow-through.
- Prioritise important client activities.
- Recover from temporary failures.
- Prevent duplicate actions.
- Monitor system health and operational usage.
- Provide simple pause and resume controls.
- Notify the user only when meaningful attention is required.

The final objective is a dependable daily operating service that keeps the CRE business moving while the user focuses on clients, relationships, negotiations and closing transactions.

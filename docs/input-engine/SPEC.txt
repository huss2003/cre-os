MODULE 10: INPUT ENGINE

Master AI Development and Implementation Prompt

Turning Everyday Information into Usable CRE Memory

1. PROJECT OBJECTIVE

Develop Module 10 – Input Engine for the CRE AI Operating System.

The Input Engine is responsible for capturing, processing, understanding, extracting, validating and organising information received from different sources and formats.

The objective is to make adding information to the CRE system effortless.

Users should be able to provide information through voice notes, typed notes, images, screenshots, business cards, documents, spreadsheets, permitted recordings and other supported formats.

The system must automatically understand the information, identify relevant CRE entities, match them against existing database records and propose the appropriate updates.

The Input Engine must convert unstructured information into structured and actionable business memory.

It must integrate with the existing modules, particularly:

- Module 1 – Database
- Module 2 – Signal Engine
- Module 3 – Scoring Engine
- Module 4 – Contact Enrichment
- Module 5 – Outreach Engine
- Module 7 – Meeting Intelligence
- Module 8 – Deal Toolkit
- Module 9 – Orchestrator
- Module 12 – Supercharged Assistant

Do not develop this as an isolated document-processing application. It must function as the central information intake layer of the CRE AI Operating System.

2. MULTI-FORMAT INFORMATION CAPTURE

The system must allow users to submit information through multiple input methods.

2.1 Text Input

Features:

- Capture manually typed notes.
- Accept informal and unstructured business notes.
- Understand incomplete sentences and shorthand.
- Understand common CRE terminology.
- Identify companies, people, buildings, locations and requirements.
- Extract dates, rent figures, areas and commercial terms.
- Identify commitments, objections and follow-up instructions.
- Convert relevant information into structured records.
- Preserve the original note.

Example input:

"Spoke to Anil. They need around 20,000 feet in Kharadi by September. Budget is 85. Send options Friday."

The system should identify:

- Contact: Anil.
- Potential requirement: approximately 20,000 SF.
- Location: Kharadi.
- Target date: September.
- Budget: 85, unit and pricing basis unknown.
- Action: Send property options.
- Due date: Friday.

It must ask for clarification about the pricing basis before treating 85 as a confirmed commercial value.

2.2 Voice Notes

Features:

- Allow users to record voice notes.
- Support uploaded audio recordings.
- Convert audio into text.
- Understand natural speech.
- Support English, Hindi and Hinglish.
- Recognise common CRE abbreviations and terminology.
- Identify names, locations, dates, amounts and commitments.
- Extract actionable information.
- Preserve the original audio file.
- Store the transcription alongside the original recording.
- Allow users to review and correct transcription errors.

2.3 Image and Screenshot Input

Features:

- Upload photographs and screenshots.
- Extract text using OCR.
- Understand business-related images.
- Identify company names and contact details.
- Extract property information from brochures and advertisements.
- Extract rent quotations and commercial terms.
- Recognise property layouts and relevant labels.
- Extract visible dates, prices, areas and specifications.
- Identify potentially relevant information in screenshots of conversations.
- Allow extracted information to be reviewed before important database updates.

2.4 Business Card Capture

Features:

- Upload business card images.
- Extract the person's name.
- Extract designation.
- Extract company name.
- Extract email address.
- Extract telephone number.
- Extract website.
- Extract office address.
- Identify the likely company and contact record.
- Match against existing contacts.
- Suggest a new contact if no reliable match exists.
- Flag uncertain fields for review.
- Preserve the original business card image.

2.5 Document Upload

Support the following document formats:

- PDF
- DOC
- DOCX
- PPT
- PPTX
- Images
- Scanned documents

Features:

- Extract document text.
- Process scanned documents using OCR.
- Identify document type.
- Extract relevant CRE information.
- Identify company and property references.
- Extract lease terms.
- Extract commercial quotations.
- Extract property specifications.
- Identify dates, amounts, areas and contractual details.
- Identify requirements and commitments.
- Preserve the original document.
- Maintain references to the original document and relevant page numbers.

2.6 Permitted Meeting Recordings

Features:

- Accept permitted meeting recordings.
- Transcribe recordings.
- Identify speakers where technically possible.
- Extract meeting-related information.
- Identify decisions and commitments.
- Extract objections and requirements.
- Identify follow-up activities.
- Preserve timestamps and transcript references.
- Send meeting-related information to Module 7 – Meeting Intelligence.

Recording and processing must follow applicable consent and privacy requirements.

3. SPREADSHEET IMPORT ENGINE

The system must support importing structured business information from spreadsheets.

Supported formats:

- Excel (.xlsx and .xls)
- CSV

Features:

3.1 Spreadsheet Upload

- Upload spreadsheets.
- Read multiple worksheets.
- Identify column headers.
- Detect data types.
- Identify blank and incomplete rows.
- Detect duplicate records.
- Identify potentially invalid values.
- Preserve the original uploaded spreadsheet.

The system should automatically identify and map spreadsheet columns to the correct database fields.

Example:

Spreadsheet Column| Suggested Database Field
Company| Company Name
Contact Person| Contact Name
Mobile| Phone Number
Office Space| Required Area
Location| Preferred Location
Rent| Expected Rent
Expiry| Lease Expiry Date

Features:

- Suggest column mappings automatically.
- Allow users to review mappings.
- Allow users to correct mappings.
- Support custom column names.
- Remember previously approved mapping patterns.
- Detect changed spreadsheet layouts.
- Identify columns that cannot be mapped.
- Report incomplete or ambiguous mappings.

3.3 Spreadsheet Validation

Features:

- Validate email formats.
- Validate telephone numbers.
- Identify inconsistent date formats.
- Identify invalid area values.
- Identify missing company names.
- Identify duplicate contacts.
- Identify duplicate companies.
- Detect suspicious numerical values.
- Identify conflicting information against existing records.
- Generate an import validation report.

3.4 Spreadsheet Change Detection

When users upload an updated version of an existing spreadsheet:

- Identify previously imported records.
- Match existing records against new rows.
- Detect newly added records.
- Detect modified values.
- Detect removed records.
- Identify changed column layouts.
- Generate a change summary.
- Propose appropriate database updates.
- Preserve historical values.
- Do not silently delete existing records because a row is missing from a new spreadsheet.

5. INTELLIGENT ENTITY MATCHING

The Input Engine must match extracted information against existing database records before creating new records.

5.1 Company Matching

Features:

- Match company names.
- Recognise different spellings.
- Recognise abbreviations.
- Identify alternate company names.
- Identify subsidiary and parent company references.
- Consider location and other identifying information.
- Detect potential duplicate companies.
- Suggest the most likely existing company record.

5.2 Contact Matching

Features:

- Match people using names.
- Match using email addresses.
- Match using phone numbers.
- Consider current and previous employers.
- Consider designations.
- Identify spelling variations.
- Detect potential duplicate contacts.
- Preserve the contact's historical employment information.

A contact changing employers must not automatically cause the contact's previous company opportunities to be transferred to the new company.

5.3 Property Matching

Features:

- Match building names.
- Recognise alternate building names.
- Match business park names.
- Identify addresses and locations.
- Detect duplicate property records.
- Suggest existing properties when a likely match is found.

5.4 Confidence-Based Matching

The system must classify matching results as:

- High confidence.
- Medium confidence.
- Low confidence.
- No reliable match.

High-confidence matches may be proposed for automatic association under configured rules.

Medium- and low-confidence matches must be presented for user review.

The system must not merge uncertain company, contact, property or opportunity records without approval.

6. DUPLICATE DETECTION

The Input Engine must detect duplicate information across uploads and existing records.

Features:

- Identify duplicate file uploads.
- Identify repeated observations.
- Detect duplicate business cards.
- Detect duplicate company information.
- Detect duplicate contact information.
- Detect repeated property quotations.
- Identify repeated meeting notes.
- Detect duplicate spreadsheet imports.
- Identify substantially identical information received from different sources.
- Preserve source history.
- Avoid creating unnecessary database records.

Repeated information must not automatically be treated as independent evidence.

The system should identify whether a new observation confirms existing information or simply repeats the same source.

---

7. DATA VALIDATION AND CONFLICT DETECTION

The Input Engine must validate information before creating or updating important database records.

Features:

- Identify missing information.
- Identify ambiguous information.
- Identify conflicting values.
- Identify questionable numerical values.
- Identify inconsistent dates.
- Identify uncertain commercial terms.
- Compare extracted values against existing records.
- Highlight differences.
- Suggest possible resolutions.
- Ask the user only for necessary clarification.

Example

Existing database record:

Monthly rent: ₹85/SF.

New uploaded quotation:

Rent: ₹95/SF.

The system must:

- Identify the difference.
- Preserve the previous value.
- Store the new quotation as a separate observation or offer version.
- Identify the source and date.
- Highlight the commercial change.
- Trigger the relevant review process.
- Avoid silently overwriting the previous information.

8. INFORMATION CLASSIFICATION AND RECORD CREATION

The system must distinguish between different types of information.

8.1 Confirmed Facts

Information directly supported by a reliable source or explicitly confirmed by the user.

8.2 Estimates

Approximate information that has not been confirmed.

8.3 Assumptions

Information inferred by the system but not directly established.

8.4 Hypotheses

Potential business interpretations that require further validation.

8.5 Commitments

Actions or obligations explicitly agreed to by a person or organisation.

8.6 Tasks

Activities that need to be completed.

8.7 Preferences

User or client preferences that may influence future recommendations.

Every important extracted item should include:

- Original source.
- Source type.
- Observation date.
- Extraction date.
- Verification status.
- Confidence level where applicable.
- Linked company, contact, property or requirement.
- Original source reference.

9. HUMAN REVIEW AND APPROVAL

The Input Engine must balance automation with accuracy.

Features:

- Automatically process low-risk information.
- Automatically save eligible internal observations under configured rules.
- Present uncertain entity matches for review.
- Request approval before merging uncertain records.
- Request review before making material commercial updates.
- Allow users to correct extracted information.
- Allow users to reject proposed updates.
- Allow users to confirm suggested records.
- Preserve the original extracted information.
- Maintain a history of user corrections.

The system must not ask users to confirm every minor extraction.

It should only request clarification when the uncertainty could materially affect the accuracy of the database or a business decision.

- Structured validation.
- Entity resolution.
- Duplicate detection.
- Human review.
- Database updates.

15. IMPORTANT BUSINESS RULES

The following rules are mandatory:

1. Preserve raw information even when extraction fails.

2. Do not silently convert carpet area into chargeable area.

3. Do not assume that a rent value includes CAM.

4. Do not assume missing commercial values are zero.

5. Do not convert an uncertain spreadsheet date into a confirmed contractual date.

6. Do not silently overwrite historical values.

7. Do not merge uncertain company, contact or property records.

8. Do not treat estimates as confirmed facts.

9. Do not treat AI interpretations as direct statements from clients.

10. Do not treat repeated information from the same underlying source as independent evidence.

11. Ask only necessary clarification questions.

12. Preserve original files, notes, images and recordings.

13. Require review for material commercial changes under configured approval rules.

14. Automatically process low-risk information only within explicitly configured permissions.

15. Provide a plain-language receipt explaining what was saved, changed, questioned or scheduled.

16. EXAMPLE END-TO-END SCENARIO

User submits:

"Spoke to Anil. They need around 20,000 feet in Kharadi by September. Budget is 85. Send options Friday."

The Input Engine should perform the following:

STEP 1: CAPTURE
Save the original note.

STEP 2: EXTRACTION
Identify:

- Contact: Anil.
- Potential area requirement: approximately 20,000 SF.
- Preferred location: Kharadi.
- Target timeline: September.
- Budget: 85, pricing basis unknown.
- Follow-up: Send property options.
- Follow-up deadline: Friday.

STEP 3: ENTITY MATCHING
Search the existing database for:

- Anil.
- His associated company.
- Existing company requirements.
- Related opportunities.

STEP 4: VALIDATION
Identify that the budget value lacks a confirmed unit and pricing basis.

STEP 5: PROPOSED UPDATES
Prepare:

- A potential requirement update.
- A proposed follow-up task.
- Links to the relevant company and contact, if confidently matched.

STEP 6: CLARIFICATION
Ask whether the budget of 85 means ₹85 per square foot per month and whether CAM is additional.

STEP 7: DATABASE UPDATE
Save confirmed and approved information with source references and verification status.

STEP 8: ORCHESTRATION
Notify Module 9 about relevant updates and proposed follow-up scheduling.

STEP 9: USER RECEIPT
Display:

- Note saved.
- Contact matched or awaiting confirmation.
- Requirement proposed.
- Budget clarification pending.
- Friday follow-up task created or awaiting required confirmation.

19. FINAL EXPECTATION

The Input Engine should make information capture almost effortless.

The user should not need to manually organise every note, read every uploaded document, enter every business card or manually transfer every quotation into the database.

The system should understand incoming information, identify what matters, connect it to the correct business records, preserve its source, identify uncertainty and turn it into usable business memory.

The final objective is simple:

CAPTURE INFORMATION ONCE. UNDERSTAND IT. VALIDATE IT. CONNECT IT. MAKE IT ACTIONABLE.

The Input Engine must become the primary entry point through which everyday CRE business information enters and improves the entire AI Operating System.
